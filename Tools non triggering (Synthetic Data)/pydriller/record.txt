Record of house at the ice
==========================

The packings are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the ice.

When the packings were reported to one decimal place the instrument
resolved only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at
the ice eventually explained it anyway.

A lot rejected at house is held, not returned the same day. The ice
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the packings were
rejected at house in the first place.

The packings are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the ice tracks is derived from the packings,
and each derivation is written down.

The oldest holding of packings sets the review date, not the largest. Age
is what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
packings problem will be.

Work not finished by the end of the opening hour at the ice is raised, not
carried quietly on. Carried quietly it surfaces at house, where it costs a
turn instead of an hour.

Kept as a plain record of the packings. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the ice.
