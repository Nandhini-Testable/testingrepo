Record of kiln at the hop
=========================

The loads are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the hop.

When the loads were reported to one decimal place the instrument resolved
only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at the hop
eventually explained it anyway.

A lot rejected at kiln is held, not returned the same day. The hop learned
that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the loads were rejected
at kiln in the first place.

The loads are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the hop tracks is derived from the loads, and
each derivation is written down.

The oldest holding of loads sets the review date, not the largest. Age is
what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
loads problem will be.

Work not finished by the end of the opening hour at the hop is raised, not
carried quietly on. Carried quietly it surfaces at kiln, where it costs a
turn instead of an hour.

Kept as a plain record of the loads. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the hop.
