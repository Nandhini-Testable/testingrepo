Glass -- what is counted, and how
=================================

The reference piece is checked against the standard; the standard is
checked against nothing. At the glass this is understood to be the weak
point of the whole arrangement for the ramps.

When the ramps were reported to one decimal place the instrument resolved
only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at the glass
eventually explained it anyway.

Annealing sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked
harder without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at annealing is
read as information rather than fault.

Annealing cannot be hurried and the glass stopped trying. Rushing yields
not a faster turn but a later one, because the ramps have to go back
through annealing.

Tolerance on the ramps belongs to the measuring method, not the turn. When
the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older ramps
figures were left as they stood.

Two people witness the frame being zeroed before the first ramps are
measured. A zero witnessed by one person has failed twice at the glass,
both times in the same direction.

Heat, draught and water at annealing sit inside a band rather than at a
target. A target invites correction toward it, and correcting toward an
unmeasured number cost the glass a week.

Conditions at annealing are logged at the start and the end of a turn,
never averaged across the middle. An average hides a glass drifting
steadily in one direction.

A record of the glass, not source. The distinction is the whole purpose of
the folder these ramps notes sit in.
