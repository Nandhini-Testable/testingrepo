Flax: standing notes on the ponds
=================================

Tolerance on the ponds belongs to the measuring method, not the turn. When
the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older ponds
figures were left as they stood.

Sequence matters more than date in the flax record. Each change to how the
ponds were handled made the next one legible, and none would have been
noticed under the earlier practice.

Conditions at retting are logged at the start and the end of a turn, never
averaged across the middle. An average hides a flax drifting steadily in
one direction.

The oldest holding of ponds sets the review date, not the largest. Age is
what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
ponds problem will be.

Loss between receipt and the close of retting is attributed, not written
off. The incoming lot is weighed before anything is done to it, which is
the one step the flax never skips.

Retting cannot be hurried and the flax stopped trying. Rushing yields not
a faster turn but a later one, because the ponds have to go back through
retting.

A holding of ponds reviewed and left in place is marked reviewed. The flax
no longer distinguishes reviewed from unreviewed by silence, having done
so for most of one season.

A record of the flax, not source. The distinction is the whole purpose of
the folder these ponds notes sit in.
