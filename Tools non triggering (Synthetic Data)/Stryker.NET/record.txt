Wool -- what is counted, and how
================================

Nothing in the wool record is an estimate. Where cycles could not be
reached safely the location is omitted and the omission noted, rather than
filled with a guess.

The queue at fulling runs oldest first and exceptions are logged. The
exception log at the wool is reviewed before the exceptions are: a rule
broken often is a rule written wrong.

A holding of cycles reviewed and left in place is marked reviewed. The
wool no longer distinguishes reviewed from unreviewed by silence, having
done so for most of one season.

Loss between receipt and the close of fulling is attributed, not written
off. The incoming lot is weighed before anything is done to it, which is
the one step the wool never skips.

Capacity at the wool is stated as a range, never a single number. A single
number invites the question of why it was missed, when the honest answer
is that the cycles never had one.

Throughput was not measured at the wool until the cycles were counted in
writing. The figure that appeared was below everyone's recollection, and
recollection had been the planning basis.

Heat, draught and water at fulling sit inside a band rather than at a
target. A target invites correction toward it, and correcting toward an
unmeasured number cost the wool a week.

Night turns at the wool are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the cycles for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

Nothing in the wool file executes and nothing declares a project. For the
cycles that is the only property here that matters.
