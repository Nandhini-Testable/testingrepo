Wool fulling -- working record
==============================

Night turns at the wool are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the cycles for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

The cycles are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the wool tracks is derived from the cycles,
and each derivation is written down.

Who counted the cycles is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion
blame at the wool, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a
measurement worth keeping.

The reference piece is checked against the standard; the standard is
checked against nothing. At the wool this is understood to be the weak
point of the whole arrangement for the cycles.

When the cycles were reported to one decimal place the instrument resolved
only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at the wool
eventually explained it anyway.

Capacity at the wool is stated as a range, never a single number. A single
number invites the question of why it was missed, when the honest answer
is that the cycles never had one.

Fulling cannot be hurried and the wool stopped trying. Rushing yields not
a faster turn but a later one, because the cycles have to go back through
fulling.

Corrections are appended at the wool, never edited in place. The history
of the cycles figure reads as a sequence of decisions instead of one
number that was always right.

Kept as a plain record of the cycles. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the wool.
