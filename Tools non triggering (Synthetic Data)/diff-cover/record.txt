Tin smelting -- working record
==============================

Night turns at the tin are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the charges for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

The charges are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the tin tracks is derived from the charges,
and each derivation is written down.

Who counted the charges is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion
blame at the tin, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a
measurement worth keeping.

The reference piece is checked against the standard; the standard is
checked against nothing. At the tin this is understood to be the weak
point of the whole arrangement for the charges.

When the charges were reported to one decimal place the instrument
resolved only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at
the tin eventually explained it anyway.

Capacity at the tin is stated as a range, never a single number. A single
number invites the question of why it was missed, when the honest answer
is that the charges never had one.

Smelting cannot be hurried and the tin stopped trying. Rushing yields not
a faster turn but a later one, because the charges have to go back through
smelting.

Corrections are appended at the tin, never edited in place. The history of
the charges figure reads as a sequence of decisions instead of one number
that was always right.

Kept as a plain record of the charges. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the tin.
