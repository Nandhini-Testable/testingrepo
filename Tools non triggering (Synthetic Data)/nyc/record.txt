Cider -- what is counted, and how
=================================

Capacity at the cider is stated as a range, never a single number. A
single number invites the question of why it was missed, when the honest
answer is that the pressings never had one.

Tolerance on the pressings belongs to the measuring method, not the turn.
When the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older
pressings figures were left as they stood.

Who counted the pressings is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion
blame at the cider, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a
measurement worth keeping.

Press sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked harder
without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at press is read as
information rather than fault.

The oldest holding of pressings sets the review date, not the largest. Age
is what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
pressings problem will be.

Night turns at the cider are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the pressings for most of a
quarter before anyone looked.

A lot rejected at press is held, not returned the same day. The cider
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the pressings
were rejected at press in the first place.

A record of the cider, not source. The distinction is the whole purpose of
the folder these pressings notes sit in.
