Net -- what is counted, and how
===============================

Mending cannot be hurried and the net stopped trying. Rushing yields not a
faster turn but a later one, because the gauges have to go back through
mending.

Conditions at mending are logged at the start and the end of a turn, never
averaged across the middle. An average hides a net drifting steadily in
one direction.

A difference between the written figure and the physical count is carried
as an open line, never absorbed. The net has closed lines three turns
after they were raised, and the gauges were right.

The gauges are the unit of account because they can be counted without
being moved. Everything else the net tracks is derived from the gauges,
and each derivation is written down.

Who counted the gauges is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion
blame at the net, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a
measurement worth keeping.

A holding of gauges reviewed and left in place is marked reviewed. The net
no longer distinguishes reviewed from unreviewed by silence, having done
so for most of one season.

The queue at mending runs oldest first and exceptions are logged. The
exception log at the net is reviewed before the exceptions are: a rule
broken often is a rule written wrong.

A record of the net, not source. The distinction is the whole purpose of
the folder these gauges notes sit in.
