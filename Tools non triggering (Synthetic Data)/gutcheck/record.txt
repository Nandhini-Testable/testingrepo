Tin smelting -- working record
==============================

Smelting sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked
harder without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at smelting is
read as information rather than fault.

Opening figures at the tin are carried forward, not re-entered. A re-
entered opening is an unlogged correction, and an unlogged correction
cannot be told from a miscount of the charges.

Corrections are appended at the tin, never edited in place. The history of
the charges figure reads as a sequence of decisions instead of one number
that was always right.

Throughput was not measured at the tin until the charges were counted in
writing. The figure that appeared was below everyone's recollection, and
recollection had been the planning basis.

Smelting cannot be hurried and the tin stopped trying. Rushing yields not
a faster turn but a later one, because the charges have to go back through
smelting.

Tolerance on the charges belongs to the measuring method, not the turn.
When the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older
charges figures were left as they stood.

The tin keeps the superseded figure alongside the corrected one for a full
cycle. A correction that erases its predecessor leaves the charges series
looking as though it never moved.

The queue at smelting runs oldest first and exceptions are logged. The
exception log at the tin is reviewed before the exceptions are: a rule
broken often is a rule written wrong.

A record of the tin, not source. The distinction is the whole purpose of
the folder these charges notes sit in.
