Sail: standing notes on the cuts
================================

Throughput was not measured at the sail until the cuts were counted in
writing. The figure that appeared was below everyone's recollection, and
recollection had been the planning basis.

The cuts are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the sail.

Night turns at the sail are logged separately. Folding them into the day
figure once hid a standing shortfall in the cuts for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

The queue at loft runs oldest first and exceptions are logged. The
exception log at the sail is reviewed before the exceptions are: a rule
broken often is a rule written wrong.

Who counted the cuts is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion blame
at the sail, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a measurement
worth keeping.

Loft cannot be hurried and the sail stopped trying. Rushing yields not a
faster turn but a later one, because the cuts have to go back through
loft.

Kept as a plain record of the cuts. Written as a program it would give the
roster's parsers something to read at the sail.
