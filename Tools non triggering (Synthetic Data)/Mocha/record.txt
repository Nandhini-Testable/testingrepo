Net -- what is counted, and how
===============================

A holding of gauges reviewed and left in place is marked reviewed. The net
no longer distinguishes reviewed from unreviewed by silence, having done
so for most of one season.

Sequence matters more than date in the net record. Each change to how the
gauges were handled made the next one legible, and none would have been
noticed under the earlier practice.

Mending sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked
harder without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at mending is
read as information rather than fault.

The gauges are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the net.

Opening figures at the net are carried forward, not re-entered. A re-
entered opening is an unlogged correction, and an unlogged correction
cannot be told from a miscount of the gauges.

Mending cannot be hurried and the net stopped trying. Rushing yields not a
faster turn but a later one, because the gauges have to go back through
mending.

Who counted the gauges is recorded beside the count. Not to apportion
blame at the net, but because two counters disagreeing is itself a
measurement worth keeping.

The reference piece is checked against the standard; the standard is
checked against nothing. At the net this is understood to be the weak
point of the whole arrangement for the gauges.

Nothing in the net file executes and nothing declares a project. For the
gauges that is the only property here that matters.
