Tin smelting: method and standing practice
==========================================

Work not finished by the end of the opening hour at the tin is raised, not
carried quietly on. Carried quietly it surfaces at smelting, where it
costs a turn instead of an hour.

Counting moved to the end of the cycle after the charges figure was
disputed twice in a month. The dispute ended; the disagreement about what
the tin was for did not.

A holding of charges reviewed and left in place is marked reviewed. The
tin no longer distinguishes reviewed from unreviewed by silence, having
done so for most of one season.

Throughput was not measured at the tin until the charges were counted in
writing. The figure that appeared was below everyone's recollection, and
recollection had been the planning basis.

When the charges were reported to one decimal place the instrument
resolved only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at
the tin eventually explained it anyway.

The queue at smelting runs oldest first and exceptions are logged. The
exception log at the tin is reviewed before the exceptions are: a rule
broken often is a rule written wrong.

Sequence matters more than date in the tin record. Each change to how the
charges were handled made the next one legible, and none would have been
noticed under the earlier practice.

Nothing in the tin file executes and nothing declares a project. For the
charges that is the only property here that matters.
