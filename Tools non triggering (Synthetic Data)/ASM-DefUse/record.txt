Saltworks -- what is counted, and how
=====================================

A holding of pans reviewed and left in place is marked reviewed. The
saltworks no longer distinguishes reviewed from unreviewed by silence,
having done so for most of one season.

Sequence matters more than date in the saltworks record. Each change to
how the pans were handled made the next one legible, and none would have
been noticed under the earlier practice.

Conditions at evaporation are logged at the start and the end of a turn,
never averaged across the middle. An average hides a saltworks drifting
steadily in one direction.

Night turns at the saltworks are logged separately. Folding them into the
day figure once hid a standing shortfall in the pans for most of a quarter
before anyone looked.

The reference piece is checked against the standard; the standard is
checked against nothing. At the saltworks this is understood to be the
weak point of the whole arrangement for the pans.

A difference between the written figure and the physical count is carried
as an open line, never absorbed. The saltworks has closed lines three
turns after they were raised, and the pans were right.

A lot rejected at evaporation is held, not returned the same day. The
saltworks learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the
pans were rejected at evaporation in the first place.

A record of the saltworks, not source. The distinction is the whole
purpose of the folder these pans notes sit in.
