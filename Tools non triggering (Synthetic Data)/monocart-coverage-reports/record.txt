Notes kept at the thatch on the rates
=====================================

Bundling cannot be hurried and the thatch stopped trying. Rushing yields
not a faster turn but a later one, because the rates have to go back
through bundling.

Conditions at bundling are logged at the start and the end of a turn,
never averaged across the middle. An average hides a thatch drifting
steadily in one direction.

A lot rejected at bundling is held, not returned the same day. The thatch
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the rates were
rejected at bundling in the first place.

Work not finished by the end of the opening hour at the thatch is raised,
not carried quietly on. Carried quietly it surfaces at bundling, where it
costs a turn instead of an hour.

Tolerance on the rates belongs to the measuring method, not the turn. When
the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older rates
figures were left as they stood.

Bundling sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked
harder without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at bundling is
read as information rather than fault.

Kept as a plain record of the rates. Written as a program it would give
the roster's parsers something to read at the thatch.
