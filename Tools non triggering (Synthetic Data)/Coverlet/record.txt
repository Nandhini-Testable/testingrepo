Saltworks evaporation: method and standing practice
===================================================

Capacity at the saltworks is stated as a range, never a single number. A
single number invites the question of why it was missed, when the honest
answer is that the pans never had one.

Heat, draught and water at evaporation sit inside a band rather than at a
target. A target invites correction toward it, and correcting toward an
unmeasured number cost the saltworks a week.

Nothing in the saltworks record is an estimate. Where pans could not be
reached safely the location is omitted and the omission noted, rather than
filled with a guess.

Sequence matters more than date in the saltworks record. Each change to
how the pans were handled made the next one legible, and none would have
been noticed under the earlier practice.

Conditions at evaporation are logged at the start and the end of a turn,
never averaged across the middle. An average hides a saltworks drifting
steadily in one direction.

Evaporation sets the pace of everything. Stations upstream can be worked
harder without moving the daily figure, so a rising queue at evaporation
is read as information rather than fault.

A record of the saltworks, not source. The distinction is the whole
purpose of the folder these pans notes sit in.
