Sail loft: method and standing practice
=======================================

A lot rejected at loft is held, not returned the same day. The sail
learned that a same-day return loses the evidence of why the cuts were
rejected at loft in the first place.

The queue at loft runs oldest first and exceptions are logged. The
exception log at the sail is reviewed before the exceptions are: a rule
broken often is a rule written wrong.

Sequence matters more than date in the sail record. Each change to how the
cuts were handled made the next one legible, and none would have been
noticed under the earlier practice.

Tolerance on the cuts belongs to the measuring method, not the turn. When
the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older cuts
figures were left as they stood.

The cuts are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the sail.

When the cuts were reported to one decimal place the instrument resolved
only to whole units. The decimal carried nothing, and someone at the sail
eventually explained it anyway.

Kept as a plain record of the cuts. Written as a program it would give the
roster's parsers something to read at the sail.
