Notes kept at the hop on the loads
==================================

Kiln cannot be hurried and the hop stopped trying. Rushing yields not a
faster turn but a later one, because the loads have to go back through
kiln.

The oldest holding of loads sets the review date, not the largest. Age is
what turns a holding into a problem; volume decides only how large the
loads problem will be.

Loss between receipt and the close of kiln is attributed, not written off.
The incoming lot is weighed before anything is done to it, which is the
one step the hop never skips.

Corrections are appended at the hop, never edited in place. The history of
the loads figure reads as a sequence of decisions instead of one number
that was always right.

Tolerance on the loads belongs to the measuring method, not the turn. When
the frame was rebuilt the tolerance moved with it, and the older loads
figures were left as they stood.

The loads are counted at the close of a turn, never at the start of the
next one. Counted late, the figure drifts with whatever happened overnight
at the hop.

Nothing in the hop file executes and nothing declares a project. For the
loads that is the only property here that matters.
